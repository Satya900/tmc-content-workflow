## Working notes

- Reader: school decision-makers for A1 and A3. Parents for A2, reached through the school.
- Job: help a school match programme format to purpose, age, time, readiness and family budget.
- Evidence: grade ranges and typical nights need TMC's own programme data (VERIFY, CLM-002). No prices, dates or availability in any asset.
- Angle: a provider telling schools a closer, shorter programme is often the right answer.
- Weakest point: without TMC's real grade ranges, panels 4 and 5 read as generic. Operations should fill the VERIFY slots before review.

Display text is written in sentence case. Staatliches renders it in capitals.

| Panel | Display text (Staatliches) | Body (DM Sans) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Cover | Day trip, domestic tour or international programme? | Which fits your school's purpose and budget |
| 2 | Start with purpose | What should students come back able to do, notice or understand? Choose the format once you have the answer. |
| 3 | Day trip | Age: suits every grade, including the youngest. Experience: one place and one theme, explored properly. Time: one school day with no nights away. Family budget: the lowest of the three. Readiness: no time away from home needed. |
| 4 | Domestic tour | Age: [VERIFY: TMC's usual grade range]. Experience: several connected places over a few days. Time: overnight stays, [VERIFY: typical number of nights]. Family budget: moderate. Readiness: comfortable spending nights away with classmates. |
| 5 | International programme | Age: older students, [VERIFY: TMC's usual grade range]. Experience: a different culture, language and daily routine. Time: the longest trip, plus passport and visa lead time. Family budget: the highest of the three. Readiness: settled with time away, unfamiliar food and routines, and travel documents. |
| 6 | How to choose | 1. Match the purpose. 2. Check time and student readiness. 3. Check what families are able to afford. When a closer programme meets the purpose, choose it with confidence. |
| 7 | Planning next year? | Set purpose, grade, dates and budget before you ask for quotations. The programme brief template is linked in the post. |

**Post copy:**

Day trip, domestic tour or international programme? The answer depends on five things more than on the destination: student age, the experience you want, the time you have, what families are able to afford and how ready students are to be away from home.

Start with purpose, then choose the format. When a closer, shorter programme meets the purpose, it is a sound decision and an easy one to explain to parents.

## A2. Instagram carousel for parents, 6 slides, 1080 × 1350

Reused on Facebook without separate production. The caption and alt text stay the same.

| Slide | Display text | Body |

| 3 | Day trip | One place, one day, home by evening. The most affordable option, and right for every age. |

**Caption:** Day trip, domestic tour or international programme. How does a school choose? It starts with what students should learn or experience. Then the school weighs age, time, readiness and what families are able to afford. Questions about your child's programme? Your school's programme coordinator is the best person to ask.

**Alt text:** Six slides explaining the difference between day trips, domestic tours and international school programmes for parents.
