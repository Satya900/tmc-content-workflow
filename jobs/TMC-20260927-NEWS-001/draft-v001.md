Hi [First Name],

Once a trip is announced, a handful of questions come up almost every time: total cost and what's included, supervision ratios, how allergies and medical needs are handled, how the school will communicate during the trip, and what the cancellation policy actually covers.

Having short, specific answers ready for these five — before the first parent asks — tends to save a lot of one-off replies later.

If you've already been through the checklist from our last email, most of these answers should already be sitting in it.

## Email 3 — What evidence to request (Day 7)

**Subject options:**
1. What to actually ask a provider to prove
2. Evidence worth requesting before you sign
3. Beyond the sales pitch: what to verify

**Preview text:** The documents and specifics worth asking for, not just the promises.

**Body:**

Hi [First Name],

Most travel providers will tell you they're safe, experienced and well-organised. The useful next step is asking what backs that up.

Worth requesting directly:
- Safeguarding policy and how it applies to this specific trip
- Insurance coverage and what it excludes
- Actual staff-to-student ratios for this group size and age
- References from another school that's travelled with them recently
- Emergency contact and escalation protocol, in writing

A provider that answers these specifically and quickly is telling you something. So is one that can't.

**CTA/destination:** Optional: reply if you'd like a short list of exact questions to send a shortlisted provider.

Here's a hypothetical worth walking through — not a real trip, just an illustration of how the checklist plays out in practice.

A school is comparing two providers for a 4-night trip. Provider A quotes a lower price but is vague on staff ratios and takes several days to answer safeguarding questions. Provider B costs more, but supplies a written ratio, a named safeguarding contact, and references from a school of similar size within a day of being asked.

Price matters, but on its own it doesn't answer the questions that actually predict how a trip goes. Running both providers through the same five-question list from Email 3 usually makes the gap obvious faster than comparing quotes side by side.
