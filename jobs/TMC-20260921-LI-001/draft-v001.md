Before a school shortlists destinations, it helps to agree on what the trip needs to do for this group of students.

Three questions belong in that first planning conversation.

1. What do we want students to explore in person?

Be specific about the learning purpose. For a history group, that might mean examining how a place remembers its past. For geography, it could mean observing how a city manages water or transport.

The purpose gives teachers a basis for choosing visits and planning what students will discuss afterwards.

2. What is manageable for this particular group?

Consider age, previous travel experience, accessibility needs and time away from home. Look at the full day, including transfers, walking, meals and rest.

A visit may fit the subject well while the journey and pace need adjustment.

3. What will families need to understand before deciding?

Discuss the total expected cost, what is included, supervision arrangements and how the school will communicate with parents. Identify which details are confirmed and which still need checking.

Those answers belong in the planning brief before families are asked to commit.

A clear brief gives the destination shortlist a purpose: a trip the school can explain educationally, assess practically and discuss openly with families.
