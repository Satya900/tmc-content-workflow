## A1. Blog (step-by-step guide)

**Title:** How to shortlist a school programme in four steps

**Slug (proposal):** `/blog/shortlist-school-programme`

**Meta description (proposal):** A four-step way to shortlist a school trip that students want to join and families consider worthwhile, before you request quotations.

---

A school programme works when three groups agree. The school, because it serves a purpose. Students, because they want to go. Families, because they consider it worth the cost. Miss one and sign-ups stall, or the trip goes ahead with half the class.

This is a four-step process we suggest for building a shortlist before you ask anyone for a quotation. Adapt it to how your school makes decisions.

### Step 1. Establish the purpose

Decide what students should come back able to do, notice or understand. Write it in one or two sentences. Note the grade, the term and anything the programme must connect to.

Purpose is the filter for every later step. A programme students love that serves no purpose is a holiday. A programme with a strong purpose that nobody joins doesn't run.

### Step 2. Gather preferences

Ask students what kind of experience appeals to them. Ask through teachers, in class, or through parents for younger grades. Ask about types of activity rather than destinations, so the answers can guide the shortlist without turning it into a popularity vote.

The activity-choice sheet linked below is one simple way to do this. It collects preferences only, with no names or contact details.

### Step 3. Assess affordability

Set a realistic budget range per student with your management, taking into account what families in your school community can pay. Decide early how payments will be spread, so the cost is presented to parents in a form they can plan for.

### Step 4. Compare a shortlist

Choose two or three options that fit the purpose, appeal to students and sit within the budget range. Send each provider the same brief and compare quotations line by line.

### Enthusiasm is not participation

A show of hands in class is a signal, not a sign-up. Before booking, ask families for an expression of interest that includes the likely cost range and payment dates. Confirm the minimum group size the price depends on. Book against confirmed numbers, not against early excitement.

### A shortlist your school can explain

When the purpose is clear, students have had a say and the cost has been tested with families, the final choice is easy to explain to management and to parents.

## A2. LinkedIn summary (TMC company page)

A school programme works when three groups agree: the school, students and families.

A four-step way to build a shortlist before asking for quotations:

1. Establish the purpose. What should students come back able to do?
2. Gather preferences. Ask about types of activity, not destinations.
3. Assess affordability. Set a budget range and payment plan with management.
4. Compare a shortlist. Two or three options, one brief, line-by-line quotations.

And remember: a show of hands is a signal, not a sign-up. Book against confirmed numbers.

## A4. Activity-choice sheet (school-shareable, printable A4 and mobile)

For teachers or parents to gather student preferences. Collects no names, contact details or TMC data.

**Title:** What kind of trip would you enjoy most?

**Instructions (for the teacher):** Hand out in class or share with parents of younger students. Collect the sheets and count the ticks. No names needed.

**Grade:** ______

**Tick up to three you'd most enjoy:**

- [ ] Visiting a museum, fort or historic site
- [ ] A science centre, planetarium or lab visit
- [ ] Nature, wildlife or a trek
- [ ] Meeting people and learning how they live and work
- [ ] Hands-on making: craft, cooking or a workshop
- [ ] Sports or outdoor challenges
- [ ] Performing arts or music
- [ ] Something else: ______________

**Which would you prefer?**

- [ ] A day trip
- [ ] A few days away with my class
- [ ] Not sure yet

**One thing you'd want to learn or see:** ______________________________
