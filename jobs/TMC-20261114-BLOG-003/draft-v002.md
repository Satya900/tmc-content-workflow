## A1. Blog post (step-by-step guide)

**Title:** How to shortlist a school programme in four steps

**Slug (proposal):** `/blog/shortlist-school-programme`

**Meta description (proposal):** A four-step way to shortlist a school trip students want to join and families consider worthwhile, before you request quotations.

A school programme works when three groups agree. The school agrees because it serves a purpose, students because they want to go, and families because they consider it worth the cost. Miss one group and sign-ups stall, or the trip goes ahead with half the class.

We suggest four steps to build a shortlist before you ask anyone for a quotation. Adapt them to how your school makes decisions.

### Step 1. Establish the purpose

Decide what students should come back able to do, notice or understand, and write it in one or two sentences. Note the grade, the term and anything the programme must connect to.

Purpose is the filter for every later step, so settle it first. Each later decision gets checked against it.

### Step 2. Gather preferences

Ask students what kind of experience appeals to them, through teachers in class or through parents for younger grades. Ask about types of activity, so the answers guide the shortlist without turning it into a vote on destinations.

The activity-choice sheet linked below is one simple way to do this. It collects preferences only, with no names or contact details.

### Step 3. Assess affordability

Set a realistic budget range per student with your management, taking into account what families in your school community are able to pay. Decide early how payments will be spread, so parents see the cost in a form they plan around.

### Step 4. Compare a shortlist

Choose two or three options which fit the purpose, appeal to students and sit within the budget range. Send each provider the same brief and compare the quotations line by line.

### Book against confirmed numbers

A show of hands in class tells you about interest. Before booking, ask families for an expression of interest with the likely cost range and payment dates. Confirm the minimum group size behind the price, and book against confirmed numbers.

### A shortlist your school explains with confidence

When the purpose is clear, students have had a say and families have seen the cost, the final choice is easy to explain to management and to parents. Each step leaves a written record to point to.

## A2. LinkedIn summary (TMC company page)

A school programme works when three groups agree: the school, students and families. Here is a four-step way to build a shortlist before you ask for quotations.

1. Establish the purpose. What should students come back able to do?
2. Gather preferences. Ask students about types of activity.
3. Assess affordability. Set a budget range and payment plan with management.
4. Compare a shortlist. Two or three options, one brief, line-by-line quotations.

Before you book, collect expressions of interest with the cost range and payment dates. Then book against confirmed numbers.
