## A1. Blog post (listicle)

**Title:** Five budget decisions to review before you cut your school programme

**Slug (proposal):** `/blog/school-trip-budget-decisions`

**Meta description (proposal):** A fixed budget and a quotation over it? Review five decisions, in order, before you cut the activities which make the trip worth taking.

The budget is fixed and the quotation is over it. The quickest cut is usually the activity list, and it is also the cut most likely to remove the reason for going.

Before you reduce the programme, review these five decisions. Each one changes the cost, and each one changes something for students. The aim is to save money where it costs the least learning.

One line stays fixed. Agreed safety requirements are never a saving option, and every change below keeps them in place.

### 1. Dates

Transport and accommodation prices vary with the season, the day of the week and local holidays. Moving the trip by a few weeks, or from a weekend to weekdays, sometimes lowers the cost without changing anything students do.

- What changes for students: check the exam calendar and the weather on the new dates.

### 2. Transport

The mode, the vehicle size and the schedule all affect cost. An overnight train in place of a flight, and a vehicle sized to the real group number, are both worth pricing.

- Effect in the worked example: [NEEDS INPUT]
- What changes for students: journey time and comfort. A long journey also takes time away from the programme.

### 3. Duration

One night fewer removes a night of accommodation, meals and staffing. It also removes a day of programme.

- Effect in the worked example: [NEEDS INPUT]
- What changes for students: less time at each place. Check which activities you would lose and whether the purpose still holds.

### 4. Distance

A destination closer to Bengaluru sometimes meets the same learning purpose for less. Heritage study, science visits and nature programmes often have strong options within a shorter journey.

- Effect in the worked example: [NEEDS INPUT]
- What changes for students: a different place. Check it serves the purpose as well as the budget.

### 5. Activities

Review activities last. Keep every activity serving the learning purpose, and remove the ones there only to fill time.

- Effect in the worked example: [NEEDS INPUT]
- What changes for students: this is the change students notice most, so cut with care.

### Why this order

Dates and transport usually change the least for students. Duration and distance change the trip itself, and activities are often the reason you are going. Work down the list and stop once you are within budget. [VERIFY: Yasin to confirm this order as TMC guidance]

### When none of the five is enough

Look at a different programme type. A strong day programme serves some purposes better than a stretched tour. Schools move between day, domestic and international programmes in both directions, and stepping down is a sound choice when it protects the purpose.
