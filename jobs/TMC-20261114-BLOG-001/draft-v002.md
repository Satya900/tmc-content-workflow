## A1. Blog post (website)

**Title:** Two school-trip quotations, two prices: when is the higher fee worth paying?

**Slug (proposal):** `/blog/compare-school-trip-quotations`

**Meta description (proposal):** Two quotations for the same school trip rarely describe the same trip. Here are seven things to compare before you decide whether the higher fee is worth paying.

Your trip committee has two quotations on the table. Same destination, same grade, same three days, and one costs Rs 4,500 less per student. Every figure below is illustrative, used to show the method.

Why would anyone pay more? It is a fair question, and sometimes the honest answer is to choose the lower price.

### The person who signs explains it twice

Whoever approves the proposal explains the choice to management, who hold the budget, and to parents, who pay the fee. "We chose the cheaper one" and "we chose the better one" both need a reason you show on paper.

### Compare prices once the trips match

Two quotations for "three days in Mysuru" often describe two very different trips. Before you compare totals, line up what each fee buys. These seven parameters cover almost every difference behind a price gap.

#### 1. Scope

Check how many full programme days the fee covers, beyond the number of nights. Check which meals are included each day. Most of all, check the group size behind the per-student price, and ask what happens to the price if only 32 of an expected 45 students sign up.

**Worth paying for:** a scope matching your plan, with no extras you didn't ask for.

#### 2. Activities

"Sightseeing" and "a facilitated planetarium session with a pre-visit worksheet" are two very different line items. Check which sites are named, whether entry tickets are included, and how much of each day is guided, facilitated or free.

**Worth paying for:** activities serving your learning purpose, with tickets and facilitation included.

#### 3. Accommodation

Check the type of property, the distance to each day's sites, how many students share a room and where teachers sleep in relation to students. A cheaper hotel an hour from every site costs you two hours of programme time a day.

**Worth paying for:** a location and room plan which save time and make supervision practical.

#### 4. Transport

Check the vehicle type and size, and whether tolls, parking and driver allowances are included. For longer journeys, check the train class or flights, and ask what happens if a vehicle breaks down.

**Worth paying for:** transport sized to the route and the group, with the extras already in the price.

#### 5. Staffing

Check who from the provider travels with the group, how many people go, and what each person is responsible for. Check how many free places are included for your teachers.

**Worth paying for:** named responsibilities, and enough people on the ground to leave your teachers free to supervise students.

#### 6. Exclusions

This is often the line which moves the most money. Look for taxes, entry fees, meals, insurance, guide fees, camera fees and "optional" activities which stop being optional once the group arrives. Add every excluded item to the lower quotation before you compare.

**Worth paying for:** a quotation where the total is the total.

#### 7. Terms

Check payment dates, the minimum group size, the conditions under which the price changes, cancellation charges and refund terms. A low price with steep cancellation charges costs families more if numbers drop after booking.

**Worth paying for:** terms your school is able to explain to parents without surprises.

### A worked example

Illustrative example. These figures are invented to show the method, and they are neither real quotations nor TMC prices.

The trip is a three-day domestic programme for 40 students in grade 8. Two providers quote for it.

| | Quotation A | Quotation B |
|---|---|---|
| Price per student | Rs 18,500 | Rs 23,000 |
| Entry tickets | Excluded, about Rs 1,200 | Included |
| Meals | Breakfast and dinner | All meals |
| Lunches for 3 days | Excluded, about Rs 900 | Included |
| GST | Plus 5%, Rs 925 | Included |
| Facilitated sessions | None | Two, with worksheets |
| Free teacher places | 1 per 20 students | 1 per 15 students |
| Cancellation | 50% from confirmation | Sliding scale by date |
| Payable per student | Rs 21,525 | Rs 23,000 |

The headline gap was Rs 4,500. The real gap is Rs 1,475, and the question is now smaller and easier to answer. Are two facilitated sessions, an extra teacher place and gentler cancellation terms worth Rs 1,475 per student for this trip's purpose? Some schools will say yes and others will say no. Either answer holds up, because the comparison is now honest.

### When the higher fee is worth paying

Pay the difference when it does one of four things. Check each one line by line.

- It serves your learning purpose.
- It removes costs parents would otherwise pay later.
- It removes work your teachers would otherwise do unpaid.
- It reduces a risk your school would otherwise carry.

### When the higher fee is not worth paying

Keep the money when the difference falls into one of these. Each is common in school-trip quotations.

- It pays for a nicer hotel your programme does not need.
- It buys extras students will not use.
- The provider is unable to show you, line by line, what the extra money buys.

### Equal value means the lower price

If the two offers deliver equal value, choose the lower price. It is a sound decision, and you will have no trouble explaining it to anyone. A provider charging more owes you a line-by-line account of what the difference buys, and the same applies to us.

### Make the next comparison easier

Comparable quotations start with a comparable brief. Send every provider the same details: grade, approximate group size, dates, purpose, must-haves and budget range. Ask each one for a line-item quotation.
