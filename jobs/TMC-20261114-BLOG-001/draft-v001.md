The trip committee has two quotations on the table. Same destination, same grade, same three days. One costs Rs 4,500 less per student.

The obvious question is why anyone would pay more. It is a fair question. Sometimes the honest answer is that you shouldn't.

### The person who signs has to explain it twice

Whoever approves the proposal will explain the choice to management, who hold the budget, and to parents, who pay the fee. "We chose the cheaper one" and "we chose the better one" both need a reason you can show on paper.

### A price only means something when the trips match

Two quotations for "three days in Mysuru" can describe two very different trips. Before you compare the totals, line up what each fee actually buys. These seven parameters cover almost every difference that moves the price.

#### 1. Scope

Check how many full programme days the fee covers, not just the number of nights. Check which meals are included each day. Most importantly, check the group size the per-student price assumes. A price based on 45 students will change if 32 sign up.

**Worth paying for:** a scope that matches your plan without extras you didn't ask for.

#### 2. Activities

"Sightseeing" and "a facilitated session at the planetarium with a pre-visit worksheet" are not the same line item. Check which sites are named, whether entry tickets are included, and how much of the day is guided, facilitated or free.

**Worth paying for:** activities that serve your learning purpose, with tickets and facilitation included.

#### 3. Accommodation

Check the type of property, how far it is from the day's sites, how many students share a room, and where teachers sleep in relation to students. A cheaper hotel an hour from every site costs you two hours of programme time a day.

**Worth paying for:** location and room arrangements that save time and make supervision practical.

#### 4. Transport

Check the vehicle type and size, whether tolls, parking and driver allowances are included, and for longer journeys, the train class or flights. Ask what happens if a vehicle breaks down.

**Worth paying for:** transport that fits the route and group size, with the extras already in the price.

#### 5. Staffing

Check who from the provider travels with the group, how many of them, and what each person is responsible for. Check how many free places are included for your teachers.

**Worth paying for:** named responsibilities and enough people on the ground that your teachers can supervise instead of administer.

#### 6. Exclusions

This is the line that moves the most money. Look for taxes, entry fees, meals, insurance, guide fees, camera fees and "optional" activities that are not optional once the group arrives. Add the excluded items to the lower quotation before you compare.

**Worth paying for:** a quotation where the total is the total.

#### 7. Terms

Check payment dates, the minimum group size, the conditions under which the price can change, cancellation charges and refund terms. A low price with steep cancellation charges can cost families more if numbers drop after booking.

**Worth paying for:** terms your school can explain to parents without surprises.

### An example, worked through

> **Illustrative example.** These are not real quotations and not TMC prices. They show the method only.

A three-day domestic programme for 40 students in grade 8.

| | Quotation A | Quotation B |
|---|---|---|
| Price per student | Rs 18,500 | Rs 23,000 |
| Entry tickets | Excluded (about Rs 1,200) | Included |
| Meals | Breakfast and dinner | All meals |
| Lunches for 3 days | Excluded (about Rs 900) | Included |
| GST | Plus 5% (Rs 925) | Included |
| Facilitated sessions | None | Two, with worksheets |
| Free teacher places | 1 per 20 students | 1 per 15 students |
| Cancellation | 50% from confirmation | Sliding scale by date |
| **Payable per student** | **Rs 21,525** | **Rs 23,000** |

The headline gap was Rs 4,500. The real gap is Rs 1,475.

Now the question is smaller and easier to answer. Are two facilitated sessions, an extra teacher place and gentler cancellation terms worth Rs 1,475 per student for this trip's purpose? For some schools, yes. For others, no. Either answer can be defended, because the comparison is now honest.

### When the higher fee is worth paying

- The difference buys something your learning purpose needs.
- It removes costs parents would otherwise pay later.
- It reduces work your teachers would otherwise do unpaid.
- It reduces a risk your school would otherwise carry.

### When it isn't

- The difference is a nicer hotel your programme doesn't need.
- It buys extras students won't use.
- The provider can't show you, line by line, what the extra money buys.

### If the two offers are equivalent, choose the lower price

That is a sound decision, and you can explain it to anyone. A provider that charges more should be able to show you exactly what the difference buys. That includes us.

### Make the next comparison easier

Comparable quotations start with a comparable brief. Send every provider the same details: grade, approximate group size, dates, purpose, must-haves and budget range. Ask for line-item quotations.
