Japan is one of the most requested destinations we're hearing from schools right now. It's also one where the planning gap shows up fastest.

Schools shortlist it for the right reasons: safety, culture, contrast with home, genuine academic value. Then the prep list turns out to be longer than a typical tour, and it lands late.

The issue usually isn't the destination. It's sequence. A few things need confirming before an itinerary gets built, not after.

1. Passport and visa lead time
Check every student's passport validity and the visa position for their nationality early. Requirements differ by country and processing can take weeks, so this can't wait until flights are booked.

2. Season before sights
Decide if the trip sits in cherry blossom season, autumn foliage, or neither before you finalise dates. Spring books out early and costs more. Autumn usually has more room to move.

3. Group documentation, ready in advance
School travel authorisation letters, parental consent, and medical or dietary declarations should exist before departure dates are locked, not chased afterward.

4. Pace, not just places
A Japan itinerary can look light on paper and heavy in practice. Rail transfers and walking distances add up fast for a school group. Build in buffer time between stops.

5. A short etiquette briefing before departure
Covering queueing norms, quiet behaviour on trains, and shoe removal at certain sites saves confusion on day one and helps students represent the school well.

6. A parent communication plan, set before wheels up
Decide in advance how and when families will hear from the group, and who they contact if something changes.

None of this is complicated. It just has to happen in order, and early.

When we scope a Japan tour for a school, these six are settled before a single destination goes on the shortlist.
