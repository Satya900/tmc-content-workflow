# TMC Brand Context

Based on: TMC content marketing approval brief (v1, 15 October 2026), Understanding The Modern Classroom guide, TMC Design Elements brand brief. Where they differ, the approval brief governs positioning, audience and content buckets. See `docs/SOURCE_DOCUMENTS.md` for provenance and open decisions.

## Business
The Modern Classroom (TMC) is an experiential education organisation based in Bangalore, India. It works with schools through day trips, domestic educational tours and international educational tours that complement classroom education.

- **Mission:** enhance traditional education by providing real-world exposure, inspiring curiosity and nurturing lifelong learning skills in students.
- **Vision:** become the go-to provider of educational tours for schools, so students experience the world as their classroom and gain skills that extend beyond textbooks.

## Core positioning
Position TMC as an **educational experience design partner**, never as a generic tour operator or travel agent. Start from the school's goals and the learners' needs. Travel is the medium for learning and student development, not the product being sold.

TMC content should show that TMC understands school goals, curriculum, student development, safety and governance, parent confidence, logistics and educational value.

## Content marketing objective
1. Build confidence in TMC.
2. Generate qualified school conversations.

Year-1 content succeeds when relevant school contacts either reach out to TMC or feel confident responding positively when TMC reaches out. Leads and qualified conversations matter more than followers.

## Audience
**Customer focus: B2B.** The school is the customer. TMC does not sell to families (B2C) or through channel partners (B2Ch). Rules: `content-strategy-workbook.md`, section 1.

Full personas, needs and emotions: `audience-personas.md`.

| Role | Who | How content treats them |
|---|---|---|
| Decision-makers (primary) | School owners, trustees, management, principals | Default reader for MARKETING content. Write to their decisions: value, risk, approval, reputation, curriculum fit |
| Influencers | Academic leaders (coordinators, HODs) and teachers | They evaluate and deliver programmes. Content helps them make the case internally and run the experience well |
| Voices | Parents and students | Their experiences and words help school leaders assess TMC's work. Parents are also an indirect influence on school decisions |

Write each asset for one primary audience.

## SEL framework used in TMC programmes
TMC frames social-emotional learning (SEL) through seven values. Use them to describe what a programme is designed to develop. Claim an outcome only with evidence (bucket 5 rules).

1. **Empathy and compassion:** engaging with local cultures and communities to understand and care for diverse people and perspectives.
2. **Self-awareness and growth:** reflection that helps students understand their emotional strengths and weaknesses.
3. **Collaboration and teamwork:** working together, resolving conflict and supporting one another towards shared goals.
4. **Mindfulness and presence:** built-in moments of reflection, gratitude and attention to the present.
5. **Lifelong learning and curiosity:** questions, critical thinking and curiosity about the world.
6. **Cultural respect and inclusion:** learning about and respecting the customs of places visited, and inclusion within the group.
7. **Emotional resilience:** handling uncertainty, managing stress and recovering from setbacks during the trip.

## Differentiators (claims, not facts)
The Understanding TMC guide lists these selling points. Each is a claim that needs evidence before it appears in published content. They are seeded as `VERIFY` rows in `claims-register-template.md`.

- Experiential, hands-on learning through historical, scientific and cultural experiences.
- Safety protocols, experienced tour directors and detailed planning.
- Programmes customised to each school's educational needs, age groups and learning objectives.
- A track record supported by testimonials from schools, parents and students.
- Holistic development: leadership, teamwork, cultural awareness and confidence.

Never write "proven track record", "numerous testimonials", "comprehensive safety protocols" or "top priority" as bare assertions. Show the process, the evidence or the sourced quote instead.

## Voice
- Warm authority, like a class teacher talking to a parent or an experienced educator talking to a principal.
- Specific over abstract. Name the place, the task, the decision.
- Practical rather than academic.
- Honest rather than promotional. If a day was long, say so.
- Experienced, calm and useful.
- Educational partner, not travel agent.

Banned words, punctuation and visual rules live in `brand-brief.md` (bundled into TMC_CONTEXT.md). They apply to every asset.

## Avoid
- "Book now", "best prices", package-selling and discount language.
- Generic phrases such as "broaden horizons" or "travel changes lives" unless made concrete.
- Unsupported superlatives and inflated educational claims.
- Academic jargon for its own sake.
- Defensive replies to objections.
- Frequent CTAs.
- Generic travel photography with no context in marketing content.
- Fear-based hooks about student safety.

## Two content modes
### TOUR
Purpose: document a real TMC trip. Photos determine the story. Copy may be light, candid, location-based and experience-led. Usual bucket: 5 (stories from the road).

### MARKETING
Purpose: build positioning, trust, authority and qualified school conversations. Strategy determines the story. Tour photos are visual evidence and emotional support. The copy carries the argument.

## Positioning sequence
Early-stage content leads with educational approach, process, safety and governance, parent confidence, planning help and real evidence of learning. Introduce deeper customisation or diagnostic language when the campaign phase and audience are ready for it.
