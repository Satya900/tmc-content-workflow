# TMC Content Generation Playbook

Based on: the Content Generation workbook (Content Delivery and Content Distribution sections), adapted for TMC's B2B school audience and evidence rules. The workbook gives B2B, B2C and B2Ch tactics for each channel. **Only the B2B tactics are used.** The answered workbook exercises are in `content-strategy-workbook.md` and ready topics in `topic-bank.md`. The 4D strategy procedure is in `marketing-template-framework.md`. Brand visuals are in `brand-brief.md`.

Workbook statistics (platform user counts, ROI figures, survey percentages) are not adopted. They are generic and undated. Never quote them in TMC content without a current, cited source.

## Content delivery = design + skills

|---|---|---|
| Colour | 2 to 3 colours chosen for brand emotion, used consistently | Paper White dominant, neutrals, Classroom Cyan accent (70 / 20 / 10) |

## Infographics
Infographic = information + graphics. It is quick to read, shareable and relatively cheap to produce.

| Type | Content | TMC use |
|---|---|---|
| Educational | Tips, tricks, do's and don'ts, facts | Main type. Planning checklists, parent briefing tips, reflection prompts |
| Inspirational | Quotes | Only real, sourced quotes with permission. Never an invented testimonial |
| Promotional | Offers, ads, service information | Programme announcements only with approved details. No discounts without approval |
| Conversational | Polls, questions, fill in the blanks, caption this | LinkedIn and Instagram polls for principals, coordinators and teachers. No consumer giveaways or contests |
| Entertainment | Puzzles, comics, memes | Rare. Teacher audiences on Instagram only. Never for owners, trustees or management |

Rules: brand colours only, include the logo, embrace blank space, less is more, use images that support the message.

## Blogs
Blogs attract search traffic and leads, update readers, build credibility and trust, and build a following through consistent useful posts.

### Blog creation template
1. **Speak about the problem.** Hook the reader with the problem.
2. **Make the problem personal.** Show what readers usually feel about it.
3. **Discovery of the solution.** How the solution was found or the reframe.
4. **Solve the problem.** The practical answer.
5. **Call to action.** Point to the TMC resource or conversation that helps further.

### Blog types and title formulas
| Type | Title formula | TMC example |
|---|---|---|
| Listicle | Number of items + benefit or statement of fact | 7 Questions to Ask Before Approving a School Trip |
| Detailed explanation | What is / What are + context | What Is Experiential Learning? A Guide for School Leaders |
| Step-by-step guide | How to + benefit + number of steps | How to Plan a Curriculum-Linked Domestic Tour in 6 Steps |

Counts in titles must match the content delivered.

## Video (scripts)
Video production is deferred. Scripts can be drafted as text assets with the templates below.

Hook timing: 3 seconds on YouTube, Facebook, LinkedIn and WhatsApp. 1 second on Instagram and YouTube Shorts.

### Informational video
1. Opening: grab attention (3 s, or 1 s on Reels and Shorts).
2. Introduction: who is speaking. Skip on Reels.
3. Seeding (about 6 s): one verified instance that earns the right to speak on the topic. Skip on Reels.
4. Value: educate, entertain or inspire, as bullet points or a story.
5. CTA: continue the conversation (comment, share, DM, answer a question).
6. Sign-off.

### Interview video
Edification (build-up about the guest) → introduction → education or inspiration → CTA → sign-off. Guests: educators, principals, subject experts. Needs the guest's consent to publish.

### Promotion video (programme or webinar)
Press the pain point → programme intro and takeaways → speaker intro → invitation and CTA → sign-off. Programme details must be approved.

### Testimonial or case study video
Hook (problem or verified result) → situation → complication → question → answer (TMC's intervention and before and after) → CTA. Evidence and consent rules from `tmc-case-study` apply.

### YouTube long-form script (8 to 12 minutes)
| Part | Length | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 3 s | A quote, statement or number (sourced) |
| Why me | 3 s | Earn the right to speak on the topic |
| Why should you care | 30 s | The cost of ignoring the problem |
| Watch-to-the-end anchor | 5 s | Seed the lead magnet CTA |
| Expand the problem | 2 min | What the problem leads to |
| Solution | 5 min | The main content |
| New reality | 2 min | What changes when the reader applies it |
| CTA | end | The right lead magnet with its link |

### Video production tips (for briefs)
Thumbnail with title and image, brand guidelines applied, plain or appealing background, look at the camera, camera at eye level, talk to the viewer rather than present, smile, tripod, light facing the speaker, a microphone, subtitles.

## Case studies and testimonials
Case studies give social proof by showing a customer's before and after, written by TMC. Testimonials are recommendations from satisfied customers in their own words. Both establish relatability, show impact, build credibility and increase conversions.

### Case study framework (SCAI)
1. **Situation:** who the school is (with permission to name, or anonymised).
2. **Complication:** the specific problem the school faced.
3. **Answer:** what TMC designed or did.
4. **Impact:** the verified outcome.

Draft template and evidence list: `tmc-case-study`.

### Testimonial types
Quote (one to three sentences), unstructured (WhatsApp messages, comments on posts), video, audio, consumer reviews (Google, social media). Each needs the author's permission for marketing use and a stored source.

### Questions that produce useful testimonials
Ask schools, teachers and parents after a programme:
1. What problem did the programme solve for you or your students?
2. What made you choose TMC?
3. Which parts of the programme worked best?
4. What made you happiest about the experience?
5. What would be the main reason you would recommend TMC to another school?

Record the answer verbatim with date, name, role and permission scope. Never edit a quote's meaning.

## Lead magnets
A lead magnet is something useful offered in exchange for contact details.

| Type | Examples | TMC fit |
|---|---|---|
| Educational | Course, webinar, tutorial | Webinar for principals on planning curriculum-linked trips |
| Solution-based | Checklist, toolkit, template | School Trip Assurance Checklist (agreed foundation asset), proposal comparison template, parent briefing pack |
| Community | WhatsApp group, Facebook group | Teacher community for trip leaders. Needs moderation and consent rules |
| Entertaining | E-book, quiz, assessment, challenge | Readiness assessment: is your school ready for an international programme? |
| Promotional | Free trial, free consultation, free design | Free programme design consultation. Needs approval before offering |

Other formats: templates, scripts, ultimate guides, tutorials, event tickets, free chapters, coupons. Coupons and discounts need commercial approval.

## Email
### High-impact email in six steps
1. Clear goal: what this email should achieve and which content achieves it.
2. Subject line: the most important part of the content, with an action word.
3. Layout: placement of copy, images and links.
4. Introduction: a few sentences that hook the reader and say what the email is about.
5. Core content: the value. Visuals such as a chart or table where useful.
6. Conclusion and CTA: short summary, links to further resources, one action.

### Email types
| Type | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Value add | Address a need or emotion and show expertise |
| Quick win | Help the reader get a small result fast |
| Free resource | Give away a useful tool or resource |
| Case study | Share a verified school story and what made it work |
| Tip of the iceberg | Share part of the content and link to the rest |
| Promotional | Persuade the reader to act now. Only approved offers |
| Engagement | Surveys, feedback requests, thank-you notes |

B2B email practice (the only practice used): segment by role, school and interaction; nurture with automated sequences; share guides and case studies; test subject lines and CTAs; follow up on engagement that did not convert. Never use false urgency.

## Distribution: channel playbooks
Paid advertising setup is out of scope for this release. These playbooks cover organic content. TMC is B2B: every channel's job is to reach a school role or help one decide. B2C tactics (shops, shopping tags, consumer discounts, user contests, cart recovery, flash sales) and B2Ch tactics (partner catalogues, channel incentives) are not used. Channel roles and tactics in one table: `content-strategy-workbook.md`, section 6.

### LinkedIn (primary B2B channel)
- Accounts: TMC company page and Yasin's personal profile. See `tmc-linkedin`.
- Content: insights, frameworks, document carousels, short videos, case studies, polls.
- Engage in education leadership groups and discussions with useful comments, not promotion.
- Webinars for principals can be promoted here.
- Track: posts, followers or connections, new connections, reach, search appearances.

### Instagram
- Role: proof and culture for school audiences. Show programme culture, preparation and real trip moments. Visual storytelling humanises TMC for principals, coordinators and teachers. Parents of client schools may see TOUR posts, but they are not the target.
- Carousels explain processes. Story highlights hold case studies, testimonials and FAQs.
- Use a small set of relevant education hashtags. Reply personally to DMs from school staff and offer a planning conversation.
- Track: posts, followers, new followers, profile visit to follow conversion, engagement rate, saves and shares.

### Facebook
- Business page with complete About, contact and website details.
- Share guides, case studies and trip documentation that school leaders can use and forward.
- Participate in educator and principal groups without overt promotion. Parent groups are not a lead source.
- Track: posts, followers, new followers, reach, engagement rate, video views.

### YouTube (deferred with video)
- Webinars, explainers, interviews with educators, trip films.
- Optimise titles, descriptions and tags. Put the lead magnet link high in the description. Pin a CTA comment. Use playlists by topic.
- Track: videos posted, average view duration, views, subscribers, new subscribers.

### WhatsApp
- Use WhatsApp Business (or the Business API at scale) with greeting, away and quick-reply messages, labels and a programme catalogue.
- Broadcast lists for school contacts who opted in. Personalised follow-ups after meetings. Status for updates.
- Never broadcast marketing to parents.
- Consent first. Parent groups during trips are operational channels, not marketing lists.
- Share square (1080 × 1080) images.

### SMS
- Reminders only: meeting confirmations, webinar reminders, post-meeting follow-ups to school contacts who agreed to receive them.
- Send in business hours. One clear action. No promotions or flash offers.

### Email
See Email above and `tmc-newsletter`. Track: database size, emails sent, open rate, click-through rate, bounce rate. Opens alone are not proof of interest.

### SEO and blog
- Keyword research from `audience-personas.md` keywords and real search data.
- On-page: keyword in title, headings, meta description and body, used naturally. Internal links between related posts.
- Off-page: quality backlinks from education sites and partner schools.
- Track: blog count, blog views, time on page, website sessions, new and returning users, exit rates on home, programmes and enquiry pages.

### Google Business Profile
- Complete profile, photos, posts and updates. Encourage genuine reviews from schools and parents and respond to each one.
- Never write or solicit fake reviews.

### X (Twitter)
- Not a lead-generation channel. Consider only after LinkedIn, Instagram, Facebook and email work consistently.
- If used: real-time education conversations, one or two relevant hashtags, promote blog posts.

### Offline distribution
School workshops and presentations, education expos and fairs, brochures and flyers, and personal meetings with decision-makers. Content assets (checklists, case studies, guides) should be printable for these.

## Choosing a platform
1. **Audience presence:** is the decision-maker there? Owners, trustees and principals: LinkedIn, email, WhatsApp, in person. Management: email, WhatsApp. Coordinators and teachers: LinkedIn, Instagram, WhatsApp.
2. **Engagement:** where does that audience actually engage?
3. **Ease of execution:** start with the channels the team can sustain.
