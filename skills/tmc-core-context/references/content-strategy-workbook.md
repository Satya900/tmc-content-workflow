## 1. Determine: customer focus

**Workbook question: "What is your customer focus? [B2C, B2B or B2Ch]"**

**Answer: B2B (business to business). The school is the customer.**

| Option | What it would mean for TMC | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| B2C (business to consumer) | Selling trips directly to parents or students | **No.** TMC does not sell to families. Parents and students take part through their school |
| B2Ch (business to channel) | Selling through distributors, resellers or franchise partners | **No.** TMC has no channel partners. Any partner (education consultant, school group office) is handled as a B2B partnership under bucket 3 |
| B2B (business to business) | Selling programmes to schools, decided by a buying committee | **Yes** |

### What B2B means in every TMC asset
1. **Reader.** The primary reader of every MARKETING asset is a school role: owner or trustee, management, principal, academic coordinator or HOD, or teacher.
2. **Parents and students are voices, not targets.** Their words, work and experiences are evidence that helps a school assess TMC. Content never asks parents or students to book, enquire or pressure their school.
3. **The account is the school.** A sale is a programme agreement with a school. A lead is a school contact. Success is a qualified conversation with a school decision-maker.
4. **Calls to action point to the school.** For example: download a planning checklist, book a programme design conversation, or forward a resource to a colleague. Never "book your child's seat".
5. **Channel tactics come only from the workbook's B2B sections.** Its B2C tactics (shops, shopping tags, consumer discounts, user contests, cart recovery, flash sales) and B2Ch tactics (partner catalogues, channel incentives) are not used.
6. **Content arms the buying committee.** A school decision involves several people, so content must help one person make the case to the others.
7. **Parent-facing material is a service to the school.** A parent briefing pack or consent explainer is made for the school to send, branded for or with the school. It is not TMC marketing to parents.
8. **TOUR content serves the client school.** Trip documentation is account care. The school shares it with its own community, and other schools read it as proof of how TMC runs a trip.

### Personas (planning fiction, not real people)
Use role names in briefs. Never present these as real schools or quotes.

**The Approver (owner or trustee).** Runs a respected school and wants it known for more than exam results. Has been offered many "educational" trips that were sightseeing tours. Approves when the risk is clearly managed, the value is clear to parents, and peers respect the choice. Wins: leadership and vision (bucket 6), governance (bucket 2), verified stories (bucket 5).

**The Champion (principal).** Believes in learning beyond the classroom but carries the risk if something goes wrong. Needs to convince the board, reassure parents and get teachers on board, all within the academic calendar. Wins: what to verify before approval (2), curriculum fit (4), planning and comparison help (7).

**The Gatekeeper (management or administration).** Wants proposals that can be compared and contracts with no surprises. Wins: transparent inclusions, timelines, responsibility matrices (7, 2).

**The Planner (academic coordinator or HOD).** Will be asked "what did they learn?" Needs objectives, preparation and reflection tasks, and evidence to report. Wins: curriculum integration (4), SEL behaviours to observe (1), evidence formats (5).

**The Trip Lead (teacher).** Will be on the ground with the students. Wants to know what is expected of them, who handles what, and that the activities are worth it. Wins: practical tools and behind-the-scenes process (4, 2). Often forwards content to the principal.

## 3. Define: content bucket research

### What would target customers love to learn before they buy?
1. How a programme connects to their curriculum and learning objectives, and how learning is captured afterwards.
2. How safety, supervision, escalation and parent communication are handled, and who is responsible for what.
3. What a programme costs, what is included, and how to judge value across proposals.
4. What other schools did and what changed for their students (verified stories only).
5. How much work falls on teachers and administrators, and what TMC handles.
6. How a programme is adapted to age group, grade and objective.

### What questions do schools have before they buy?
1. How are programmes structured, from learning objective to field task to reflection?
2. What does it cost, what is included, and what return should we expect?
3. How is student learning measured or evidenced?
4. Who are the facilitators and tour directors, and what are their qualifications? `VERIFY`
5. How does this fit our curriculum and timetable?
6. What safety measures, supervision ratios and emergency procedures are in place? `VERIFY`
7. What do our teachers have to do before, during and after?
8. How will parents be informed and consent managed?
9. What insurance, permissions and documents are covered? `VERIFY`
10. Which schools have you worked with, and can we speak to one? `VERIFY` and permission needed
11. What happens if plans change (weather, illness, cancellation)? `VERIFY`

Each question is a ready content topic. Questions marked `VERIFY` need TMC's confirmed process or an approved claim before the answer describes TMC. General guidance ("what a school should ask any provider") needs no claim.

### Top 5 topics TMC would love to educate schools on
1. Experiential learning and SEL: what it develops and how teachers can observe it.
2. Turning a field experience into curriculum learning: objective, preparation, field task, reflection, assessment.
3. Safety, governance and parent confidence in school programmes.
4. Measuring and evidencing the impact of educational programmes.
5. Planning and choosing programmes: budgets, workload, approvals and comparing providers.

### Top product lines
1. **Day trips** (local field trips). Easy first purchase for a new school.
2. **Domestic educational tours.** High value for the effort.
3. **International educational tours.** Primary product. Needs the most trust.

Across all three, the service TMC sells is **programme design**: shaping the experience around the school's objectives. Content should sell that thinking, not the destination.

### Top 10 keywords (B2B)
1. experiential learning
2. educational tours for schools
3. school field trips Bangalore
4. curriculum-linked field trips
5. international educational tours for students
6. social-emotional learning (SEL)
7. student safety on school trips
8. school trip planning
9. hands-on learning
10. learning outside the classroom

These are candidates. Check them against real search data before an SEO brief commits to them. Persona keyword lists are in `audience-personas.md`.

Use fears and pain points to name a problem honestly, then answer it practically. Never use them to frighten, and never make TMC the rescuer.

## 5. Answers to the delivery questions

**Theme: "Does this show what the brand stands for?"** It does when the asset shows TMC thinking like an educator: an objective, a process, a piece of evidence or a decision made carefully. It does not when it looks like a travel brochure.

**Theme: "Is this the memory we want people to have of TMC?"** The memory a school leader should keep is: "they understood our goals and made the decision easier and safer." Every asset should leave that impression, not "nice photos".

**Design elements.** Already decided in `brand-brief.md`. The workbook's advice to outsource design is met by the brand brief and the renderer.

**Case study template.** Use the workbook's **B2B case study template** only, adapted for schools in `tmc-case-study`. The B2C template is not used.

**Which video types.** Educational (informational, behind the scenes, interview, live Q&A for school leaders) and credibility (school testimonial and case study). Promotion videos only for an approved programme or webinar. "Lifestyle" videos only as behind-the-scenes process, never as travel lifestyle.

**Which lead magnets.** Solution-based (checklists, templates, toolkits) and educational (webinars, guides) first, because they serve a busy decision-maker. See the per-bucket list in `topic-bank.md`.

## 7. The Understanding TMC guide, read as B2B

Some lines in the guide mention parents and students as marketing targets. Read them this way:

| Guide says | B2B reading used in this system |
|---|---|
| Lead generation: "engage with school decision-makers, parents, and students to generate inquiries and bookings" | Leads are school contacts. Parent and student engagement supports a school's decision and is never a lead source |
| Email: "regular newsletters to schools and parents" | Newsletters go to school contacts. Parent communication is a service TMC provides through the school |
| Promotional content: "special offers and promotions to encourage bookings" | Only school-level programme offers with commercial approval. No consumer discounts |
| Engaging content: "quizzes, polls, and contests" | Polls and quizzes for educators. No student or parent contests used to generate leads |
| Online advertising: "Google Ads and Facebook Ads targeting schools, parents" | Paid ads are out of scope this release. If added later, target school roles only |
| Secondary audience: parents as "influencers who support and enable school decisions" | Kept. Parents are voices and indirect influence, reached through the school |
| KPI: "feedback from schools, parents, and students" | Kept. Feedback is collected for and with the client school, and becomes evidence only with permission |
