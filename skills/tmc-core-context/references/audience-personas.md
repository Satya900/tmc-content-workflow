# TMC Audience Personas

Based on: Understanding The Modern Classroom guide (customer profiling, content bucket research, needs and emotions tables). Audience roles follow the approval brief (15 October 2026). These are planning personas, not facts about any real school or person. Never quote them as research findings.

## Customer focus: B2B
TMC is B2B: the school is the customer. Not B2C (selling to families) and not B2Ch (selling through channel partners). The full answer and its rules are in `content-strategy-workbook.md`, section 1.

## Segments
| Segment | Who | Role in a decision | Can be an asset's target audience? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary | School owners, trustees, management, principals, administrators | Decide and approve | Yes |
| Primary (influencers) | Academic coordinators, HODs, teachers | Evaluate, champion internally, deliver | Yes |
| Secondary (voice) | Parents | Influence and enable school decisions. Their voices help schools assess TMC | No. Voice and evidence only |
| Tertiary (voice) | Students | End beneficiaries. Their voices and work are evidence of learning | No. Voice and evidence only |

Parent and student profiles below are kept so writers understand the people whose experience schools care about. They are never a sales target. Parent-facing material (a briefing pack, a consent explainer) is made for the school to send, so its audience in the job record is still the school role who commissions it, usually the principal.

The buying committee, B2B account profile, role-by-role psychographics and personas (Approver, Champion, Gatekeeper, Planner, Trip Lead) are in `content-strategy-workbook.md`, section 2. Needs and emotions for each of the seven buckets are in section 4.

## Needs and emotions (Decode)
Six categories, from negative to positive: Fears, Pain Points, Problems, Needs, Desires, Fantasies. Pick one for each asset (the `customer_emotion` field). The emotion must belong to the asset's school-role audience. Use the per-bucket tables in `content-strategy-workbook.md`, section 4, first. The parent and student columns below explain what school leaders must answer for; they are not emotions to target directly.

| Category | Question | School leaders and educators | Parents | Students |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fears | What worries them? | Student safety during trips. ROI of programmes. Disruption to the academic schedule | Child's safety and well-being. Value for money. Missing schoolwork | Being away from home. Not enjoying activities. Getting lost or separated from the group |
| Pain points | What frustrates them? | High trip costs. Logistical challenges. Inflexible programmes | Financial strain of extra expenses. Coordination and planning. Lack of information | Long travel times. Boredom during travel. Strict schedules |
| Problems | What obstacles do they face? | Limited budget for educational programmes. Ensuring educational value. Gaining parental approval | Balancing work and family. Trusting the programme's value. Safety concerns | Peer pressure. Adjusting to new environments. Keeping up with schoolwork while travelling |
| Needs | What is a necessity? | Assurance of student safety. Cost-effective programmes. Seamless curriculum integration | Clear communication and updates. Affordable pricing. Educational benefits | Feeling safe and secure. Enjoyable activities. Making new friends |
| Desires | What would they love to have? | High-quality educational content. Positive feedback from students and parents. Strong partnerships with educational providers | Holistic development for their child. Opportunities to excel. A strong sense of community | Fun, exciting experiences. Popular destinations. Engaging, interactive learning |
| Fantasies | What would be a dream come true? | Recognition for innovative educational programmes. Becoming a leader in experiential learning. Seamless execution without issues | Seeing their child thrive. Complete trust in the programme's benefits. Long-lasting positive impact | The best trip ever. Sharing amazing stories with friends. Capturing memorable moments |

Use fears and pain points to open a problem honestly, never to frighten. A fear hook must lead to a practical answer, not to TMC as the rescuer.

## What each segment wants to learn before buying
The school-leader column drives content. Parent and student questions are questions the school has to answer for its community, so they become content for the school (for example "the ten questions parents will ask you").

| | School leaders and educators | Parents | Students |
|---|---|---|---|
| Love to learn about | Benefits of experiential learning. How TMC complements classroom education. Success stories from other schools | Safety measures and protocols. How programmes support child development. Stories from other parents | Fun and educational value of trips. Activities included. Making new friends |
| Questions before buying | How are programmes structured? What is the cost and ROI? How is student progress measured? What qualifications do the facilitators have? How do programmes fit the current curriculum? | What safety measures are in place? What is the cost and payment structure? What are the duration and schedule? Who supervises the children? What will my child gain? | Will I enjoy the activities? Will I learn something new? Are my friends joining? What can I bring? What are the cool places we will visit? |
| Top topics to educate on | Experiential learning and its benefits. Trends in educational trips. Integrating real-world experiences into the curriculum. Measuring the impact of educational programmes. Case studies of successful tours | Importance of experiential learning for child development. Supporting a child's learning journey. Balancing fun and education. Safe travel tips for children. Stories from other parents | Making learning fun. Science facts and discoveries. Teamwork and collaboration. Curiosity and asking questions. Capturing memories through journaling |

Each buyer question is a ready content topic. Answering one honestly, with TMC's actual process, is usually stronger than a generic tip list. Questions about cost, ROI, measurement, qualifications and supervision touch claims: check the claims register first.

## School segments for prioritisation (internal)
Used to weight targeting and campaign planning. Never name a real school's segment in content. Fee figures are annual, in Indian rupees.

| Segment | Profile | Effort and return | Content priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Established multi-branch | 3+ branches, 2,000+ students, fees 1 lakh and above. Reputation for academics and holistic development. Proactive, resourced decision-makers | Low effort, high return | Highest. Leadership, curriculum and governance depth |
| B. Growing differentiator | 1 to 2 branches, 1,000 to 2,000 students, fees 1 lakh and above. Growing fast, wants to stand out | High effort, high return | High. Competitive edge, planning and decision help, proof |
| C. Small local | 1 branch, under 1,000 students, fees 75,000 to 1 lakh. Limited resources, basic focus | Low effort, low return | Low. Day trips as an entry point |
| D. Not a fit | High effort, low return | Not targeted | None |

The source guide names these Amazing, Breadwinning, Convenience and Dangerous schools. Use the neutral labels above in any file that might be shared.
