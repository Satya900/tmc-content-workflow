## 1. Agreed direction
- **Market:** year one targets **Bengaluru schools**.
- **Success:** qualified school enquiries, meetings and programme opportunities. Not reach, not downloads.
- **Programmes:** day, domestic and international programmes all remain available routes. Schools move between them in **both directions** (a school may step up from day trips or step down from an international plan). Never present the programmes as a fixed ladder.

## 2. Audience for this bucket
| Level | Who | How content reaches them |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | Owners, board members, principals, administrators and coordinating teachers **with actual authority, influence and programme need** | Website, LinkedIn, school emails, follow-up by Nadeem |
| Secondary | Parents and guardians who **fund participation, give consent and judge affordability and suitability** | Parent-facing versions supplied to the school, or school-led sharing (parent WhatsApp groups, school email), Instagram, Facebook |
| Tertiary | Students whose interests, readiness and experience influence participation | Only through school and parent routes. Never collected as sales leads |

Rules:
- School decision-makers come first in this campaign. This overrides the brand brief's parent-first wording ("like a class teacher talking to a parent") for bucket 1 assets aimed at school buyers. Parent-facing versions keep the plain, parent-readable style.
- Parent-facing material directs questions to the **school coordinator**, not to TMC sales.
- Student material invites preferences. It never collects student contact details.
- Personal age, income, lifestyle and hobbies of these audiences are **unspecified**. Do not invent them.

## 6. The five topics (agreed shortlist)
Topic IDs are permanent. Use them in job records, comments and follow-up messages. The formats and channels are **recommendations for the selection meeting**, not a performance ranking.

### B1-01. Two school-trip quotations, two prices: when is the higher fee worth paying?
- **Decode:** Problems · **Approach:** 23, parameters to consider
- **Useful answer:** compare equivalent scope, activities, accommodation, transport, staffing, exclusions and terms. Explain which differences justify extra expenditure. A higher price alone proves nothing.
- **Required input:** two actual anonymised proposals, or a clearly labelled illustrative example. Compare like-for-like assumptions.
- **Proposed first commission.** It addresses the reported price objection. If comparable offers deliver equivalent value, choosing the lower price is reasonable, and the content should say so. TMC must substantiate any claimed difference.
- **Recommended delivery:** detailed explanation blog, plus an informational comparison video.
- **Recommended distribution:** website blog with the YouTube video embedded. Short native extract on Yasin's LinkedIn. Nadeem shares the article by email with schools evaluating proposals.

### B1-02. Day trip, domestic tour or international programme: which fits your school's purpose and budget?
- **Decode:** Needs · **Approach:** 20, options to choose between
- **Useful answer:** use student age, intended experience, available time, family budget and readiness to explain when each option fits.
- **Required input:** verified programme examples and realistic scope. Do not invent live prices or availability.
- **Recommended delivery:** educational comparison infographic.
- **Recommended distribution:** TMC LinkedIn document post for school buyers. Parent-friendly Instagram carousel. WhatsApp sharing during school planning discussions. Facebook reuses the parent carousel.

### B1-03. Before parents pay: seven cost and booking questions your school should settle
- **Decode:** Needs · **Approach:** 21, questions to ask before
- **Useful answer:** clarify (1) total payable cost, (2) exclusions, (3) payment dates, (4) minimum group size, (5) price-change conditions, (6) cancellation charges, (7) refund terms.
- **Required input:** current TMC and supplier terms. Explain what to ask without implying one policy applies to every programme.
- **Recommended delivery:** educational checklist infographic.
- **Recommended distribution:** TMC LinkedIn and Instagram. A mobile-readable version supplied to schools for parent WhatsApp groups or school email. Facebook reuses the parent version.

### B1-04. A fixed school-trip budget: what should you change first?
- **Decode:** Needs · **Approach:** 4, tips
- **Blog adaptation:** "Five budget decisions to review before reducing your school programme" (listicle).
- **Useful answer:** explain trade-offs involving distance, duration, dates, transport and activities. Show the effect of each saving. Preserve agreed safety requirements.
- **Required input:** one realistic costed example approved by operations. No unsupported savings claims.
- **Recommended delivery:** listicle blog, plus an informational trade-off video.
- **Recommended distribution:** website and YouTube. Yasin explains one trade-off on LinkedIn. Nadeem uses the resource when a buyer raises budget constraints.

### B1-05. How to shortlist a school programme students want to join and families consider worthwhile
- **Decode:** Desires · **Approach:** 14, process
- **Blog adaptation:** "How to shortlist a school programme in four steps" (step-by-step guide).
- **Useful answer:** (1) establish purpose, (2) gather preferences, (3) assess affordability, (4) compare a shortlist. Separate initial enthusiasm from confirmed participation.
- **Required input:** actual programme options. Label the proposed school process clearly as guidance.
- **Recommended delivery:** step-by-step blog, plus a student-preference adaptation.
- **Recommended distribution:** website, LinkedIn summary, Instagram carousel, and a school-shareable activity-choice sheet. Student input flows through teachers or parents.

### B1-LM. Programme selection and comparison kit (lead magnet)
- **Type:** solution-based.
- **Contents:** a programme brief, a side-by-side quotation worksheet, and the seven booking questions (from B1-03).
- **Form:** name, school, role, work email. Offer an optional programme discussion **separately**, not as a required field.
- **Parent resources stay freely accessible** (not gated).
- **Placement:** website download page, linked from relevant posts, articles, video descriptions and school emails.
- A download signals interest, **not** a qualified opportunity.

## 7. Production guide (use only for selected items)
These structures turn each selected item into finished assets with the format skills. Every bracketed input must come from a real source. If it is missing, mark `NEEDS INPUT` and stop that section; never fill it with an invented figure.

### B1-01 assets
**Blog (detailed explanation, `tmc-blog`):**
1. The problem: a school holds two quotations for a similar trip at different prices.
2. Make it personal: the committee has to explain the choice to management and parents.
3. The reframe: a price is only comparable when the scope is equivalent.
4. The answer: one section per parameter: scope, activities, accommodation, transport, staffing, exclusions, terms. For each, say what to check, how it changes cost, and when the difference justifies paying more.
5. A comparison table built from [two anonymised proposals] or a labelled "Illustrative example: figures are not real quotes".
6. The honest conclusion: if both offers deliver equivalent value, the lower price is a reasonable choice.
7. CTA: the B1-LM kit (quotation worksheet), or a programme discussion.
- Do not say or imply TMC is the better-value option unless a substantiated claim is approved.

**Video script (informational, `tmc-reel`):** opening hook on two prices in 3 seconds. Introduction and seeding only with Yasin's approved credentials. Value: the seven parameters as bullets. CTA: the kit link in the description. Embed on the blog.

**LinkedIn (Yasin, `tmc-linkedin`):** one parameter, explained in Yasin's judgment. His views and examples must come from his notes. `NEEDS INPUT: Yasin's view or example` if none are supplied.

**Email (Nadeem to a school evaluating proposals, `tmc-newsletter`, one-to-one):** short, personalised to [school, contact, stage], links the article and kit, one question to move the conversation forward. No pressure language. Not a bulk send.

### B1-02 assets
**LinkedIn document post (school buyers, `tmc-marketing-carousel`):**
- Cover: the question.
- One panel each for day trip, domestic tour and international programme, with the same five rows: student age, intended experience, time needed, family budget level (relative, not prices), readiness.
- A "how to choose" panel.
- CTA panel.

**Instagram carousel (parent-friendly, `tmc-social-post` or `tmc-marketing-carousel`):** the same comparison in parent language. Ends with "Ask your school's programme coordinator". Facebook reuses it.

**WhatsApp version:** a single square image or a short sequence for school planning discussions.

Rules: no live prices, dates or availability. Programme examples must be verified.

### B1-03 assets
**Checklist infographic (school buyers, `tmc-social-post`):** title, the seven questions, and one line on why each matters.

**Mobile parent version:** the same seven questions framed as "questions your school will answer for you", directing parents to the school coordinator. Supplied to the school for parent WhatsApp groups or school email.

Rules: explain what to ask. Never state a single cancellation, refund or payment policy as if it applies to every programme. Any statement of TMC's own terms needs current terms verified by operations.

### B1-04 assets
**Listicle blog ("Five budget decisions to review before reducing your school programme", `tmc-blog`):** one decision each for distance, duration, dates, transport and activities. For each: the trade-off, what it saves in [the approved costed example], and what it changes for students. A fixed line that agreed safety requirements are not a saving option.

**Trade-off video script (`tmc-reel`):** one budget scenario, walked through decision by decision.

**LinkedIn (Yasin):** one trade-off in his voice, from his notes.

**Nadeem resource:** link plus one-line context, for when a buyer raises budget constraints.

Rules: savings figures only from the operations-approved example. No "save up to X%" claims.

### B1-05 assets
**Step-by-step blog ("How to shortlist a school programme in four steps", `tmc-blog`):** purpose, preferences, affordability, shortlist comparison. Include a section on separating early enthusiasm from confirmed participation. Label the process as guidance.

**LinkedIn summary and Instagram carousel:** four steps, one per panel.

**Activity-choice sheet (school-shareable):** a printable or mobile sheet that teachers or parents use to gather student preferences. It contains no TMC data capture and no student contact fields.

### B1-LM assets (`tmc-lead-magnet`)
The kit, its landing page copy, form fields (name, school, role, work email), a separate optional discussion request, thank-you copy and delivery email. The parent-facing parts are published ungated.

### Case study (deferred, `tmc-case-study`)
"How one school chose its programme within a fixed budget." Needs the actual brief, options, decision, TMC's contribution, specific feedback and permission. Do not draft beyond a template until those exist.
